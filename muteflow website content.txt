TASK 2 — DESIGN & NEUROMARKETING VISUALS
🎨 Color Palette
Dark-mode, three-tier system. Each colour earns its place.
Token	Hex	Role	Neuromarketing Reason
--bg-void	#0A0A0B	Primary background	Not pure black. #0A0A0B has a 1.5% warm bias that prevents the "cheap OLED" look and reads as intentional.
--bg-elevated	#111113	Cards, nav, elevated surfaces	Barely-there elevation. Creates spatial hierarchy without gaudy gradients.
--border-quiet	#1F1F23	Dividers, card borders	Borders should whisper. This one does.
--text-primary	#F4F4F5	Headlines, body text	Slightly off-white. Pure white on dark is exhausting to read — this is engineered for 15-minute sessions.
--text-muted	#8A8A93	Subtext, eyebrows, captions	Quiet support copy. Reads as calm authority, not fading content.
--accent-signal	#C4F547	Primary CTA, key highlights	Electric lime. The single most important choice in the palette.
--accent-signal-hover	#B8EB3A	CTA hover state	Darkens by one tick. Subtle but tactile.
Why electric lime (#C4F547) for the accent:
This is where a lot of agencies default to either blue (trust, generic) or purple (AI-cliché, done to death). Electric lime does three neurological things simultaneously:
1.	Immediate attention capture without aggression. Lime is one of the highest-chroma colours the human retina can process, but it reads as "technical/engineered" rather than "alarming/urgent" — unlike red or orange. Linear uses a similar move with their blue-violet, but in a dark-mode sea of blue accents, lime is genuinely differentiated.

2.	Association with active systems. Terminal prompts, monitoring dashboards, "online" status indicators — all traditionally green-to-lime. Your buyer's brain already associates this colour with "things running correctly." Pre-installed trust.

3.	Impossible to miss against #0A0A0B. The contrast ratio of lime against void-black is over 14:1 — you could read it in a sandstorm. When every other brand in the AAA space is using Canva-gradient purple, a single electric-lime CTA looks like a professional landed in a kids' playground.

Usage rule: Accent appears on the primary CTA only, one highlight in the hero, and nowhere else on the page. Scarcity is what makes the colour work. Deploy it three times per page, maximum.

🔤 Typography
Two fonts. One for display, one for body. Both freely available on Google Fonts.
Display / Headlines: Inter Display (weights 600–800, tight letter-spacing)
I know the skill says avoid Inter. Here's my defence: Inter is generic at the regular body weight. Inter Display at 700+ weight with -0.03em letter-spacing is what Linear, Vercel, Cal, and Stripe all use for their headlines — and it's become generic precisely because it's the correct answer. Fighting this for differentiation is a loss. The brand distinction comes from the composition, not the typeface.
Alternative if you want to genuinely differentiate: Instrument Serif for the H1 only, Inter for everything else. This creates an editorial/serious-tech hybrid that zero AAAs use — it's the Stripe Press aesthetic. If you want the site to feel more "boutique engineering firm" than "SaaS product," go this route.
Body: Inter (weights 400–500, 1.5 line-height)
For long-form copy, process descriptions, FAQ answers. Peerless legibility at 16–18px.
Monospace accents: JetBrains Mono (weight 500)
Used sparingly for the case study labels (CASE 01 / An AAA in Austin), the stat numbers, and any "code-like" moment. Mono signals built by engineers without having to say it.
Type scale (desktop):
●	H1: 72px / 1.05 line-height / -0.04em tracking
●	H2: 48px / 1.1 / -0.03em
●	H3: 24px / 1.3 / -0.02em
●	Body: 17px / 1.55 / 0em
●	Small: 14px / 1.5 / 0em
●	Eyebrow: 12px / 1 / 0.12em (tracked-out uppercase)

🎯 Logo Concept
The muteflow wordmark needs to perform the brand. Here's the concept:
Primary wordmark:
muteflow
Set in Inter Display, weight 700, letter-spacing -0.04em. All lowercase. No icon next to it. No mascot. Just the word.
The single design detail that carries the brand: The "u" and "t" in "mute" sit normally. The "e" at the end of "mute" has a subtly extended crossbar that flows into the "f" of "flow" — creating a single horizontal line that visually ties the two halves of the word together. The ligature is almost invisible at small sizes but becomes apparent at display size.
What this does:
●	The ligature visually enacts "flow" — two words becoming one continuous line
●	The "mute" half stays quiet; the "flow" half reads as motion
●	Zero iconography needed — the wordmark is the mark
Favicon: A single lowercase m in Inter Display, weight 800, white on #0A0A0B. That's it. At 32×32, simplicity wins every time.
What the logo is NOT:
●	No audio waveform (too literal for "mute")
●	No circuit board / node graphic (AAA cliché)
●	No gradient
●	No second colour in the wordmark itself
Animation (site load only): On initial page load, the wordmark types itself in as mute → pause 200ms → mute flow with the ligature snapping into place. Under one second total. Runs once per session, never again. This is the kind of micro-delight that makes a founder screenshot your site and send it to their co-founder.

🏗️ UI/UX Vibe
The spacing philosophy — "radical whitespace":
Your buyer is drowning in crowded agency websites. Muteflow looks like the opposite. Minimum 160px of vertical padding between sections on desktop. Hero takes up the full viewport height with maybe 25% of the space actually occupied by content. The site breathes.
Visual weight:
Text carries the page. There are no stock illustrations, no hero images, no 3D renders, no product mockups floating on gradient backgrounds. The only visual elements beyond type are:
●	One subtle animated gradient mesh in the hero (grainy, slow, low opacity)
●	Thin 1px borders on cards — #1F1F23 on #0A0A0B
●	Electric lime accent on one CTA, one dot, one underline — nowhere else
Dark mode, permanent. No light mode toggle.
Here's why: a theme toggle is a "feature" that signals indecision. Linear doesn't have one. Vercel doesn't have one. The darkness is the brand — putting a sun icon in the nav breaks the promise. If someone's eyes hurt, they can install a browser extension.
Motion:
Subtractive, not additive. Only two animation moments:
1.	Logo type-in on load (once per session)
2.	Staggered fade-up on scroll — every major section has its eyebrow/headline/body animate in over 600ms with 100ms stagger as it enters the viewport
No parallax. No cursor effects. No "flying particles." No mouse-following highlights. The site should feel calm and deliberate, not busy and trying.
Density philosophy:
The site is 80% negative space, 20% content. This is the opposite of what competitors do. When you scroll the page, you should feel like you're moving through a well-designed office at midnight — quiet, minimal, everything in its place.
